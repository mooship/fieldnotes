South Africa goes to the polls for local government elections on 4 November. Nobody gets excited about a municipal election. There's no single face on the ballot, no national drama, just a long list of wards and councillors most people couldn't name if you asked them. But this is the vote that decides whether your bins get collected on the day they're supposed to, whether the water actually comes out of the tap, whether the pothole outside your house gets fixed before it takes out your suspension. Central government barely touches your life compared to this.

Tshwane's had so many mayors since the coalition here first fell apart that I've genuinely lost count. Every time it happens the explanation is some reshuffling of the same six parties, and every time the actual problems -- refuse collection, water infrastructure that's older than I am, roads nobody's maintained properly in years -- just sit there, untouched, waiting for the next reshuffle. I don't think Pretoria is even the worst-run metro in the country. That's the part that worries me more, not less.

Five years is a long run for a council to coast on. It's long enough for whoever's in charge to bet that nobody's really watching closely enough to punish them for it come the next election, and mostly they've been right. Coalition politics since 2021 gave us metros where it's easy for every party to blame every other party and easy for none of them to actually answer for anything. I used to think that instability was a reason to switch off. Now I think it's the opposite -- a council that's already split down the middle is exactly where a few thousand extra votes can actually move something.

No party fixes a city on its own, and one election won't undo a decade of underinvestment in a single term. Council seats still get decided ward by ward, though, by whoever actually shows up on the day, and a bad council staying in charge has never needed anything from the rest of us except silence.

Check your registration, find your voting station, and go vote on 4 November. It won't fix everything. It's still the one that's actually yours to fix.
